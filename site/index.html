the problem

Your usage bill is probably a little bit wrong, and you can’t prove otherwise.

Every usage-based product counts events, runs some pricing logic, and produces a number. Then everyone just trusts the number. But between a raw event and an invoice line sits a meter, a price tier, proration, rounding, maybe a manual edit. Any of them can make the bill drift from the truth — and you find out from an angry customer, not your own system.

smolbill adds the missing step: a reconciliation ledger that replays every invoice from the raw events and proves they still agree, with a hash. If they ever drift, it shows you the exact line and why.

how it works

The AI sets the rule. The engine does the math. The ledger proves it.

# your agent passes intent over MCP — never money attach_plan(customer, plan) → ok record_usage(customer, "tokens", 15000) # the deterministic engine computes every cent finalize_invoice(subscription) → $64.00, hash 64edfeb7… # and it proves itself, any time reconcile_invoice(invoice) → consistent ✓ (meter and invoice provably agree)

A hallucinated decimal in a bill ends a business relationship — so the model is structurally kept away from the arithmetic.

why we exist

Stripe and Dodo have billing. So why us?

Because for a payment processor, billing is a feature that taxes payment volume — not a product. It’s built just deep enough to keep your money on their rails. The proof: Stripe, with the most mature billing of all of them, paid ~$1B to buy Metronome rather than fix its own billing for usage-based pricing. The dedicated billing layer exists because billing is a separate, hard problem that conflicts with a processor’s incentives.
